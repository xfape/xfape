The shift

The workforce is about to be hybrid. The infrastructure isn't.

For thirty years we've optimized enterprise software around one assumption: a person is the one acting. That assumption is quietly breaking.

Today

Built for humans

  • One identity = one employee with a badge
  • Access controlled by who you are and what you click
  • Judgment and accountability live in a person's head
  • Work happens through screens, dashboards and forms
→
Next

Built for humans + agents

  • Agents that act on behalf of a person or a team
  • Access granted by policy, scope and intent — not a login screen
  • Accountability enforced by the system, logged on every action
  • Work happens through APIs, tools and approvals

The problem

You can't just give an agent a login.

The moment a corporate tries to put an agent to work on real systems, four gaps open up — and in banking, each one is a compliance event waiting to happen.

🪪

Identity

Agents aren't people, but they aren't service accounts either. They act on behalf of someone, with delegated and scoped authority that today's IAM can't express.

Human: SSO, MFA, one badgeAgent: verifiable, delegated, revocable identity
🛂

Permissions

A human has judgment and a job description. An agent needs hard guardrails: what it may touch, how much it may spend, how fast it may move, when it must stop and ask.

Human: trusted to use discretionAgent: policy, limits, human-in-the-loop
🔌

Connection

Core banking, ERP, CRM, ticketing — built for screens, not for machines. Agents need governed, structured access to systems that were never designed to be driven by software.

Human: dashboards and click-flowsAgent: tools, APIs, structured context
🧾

Accountability

When an agent moves money or changes a record, you must answer: which agent, on whose authority, was it allowed, can we reverse it, who signs off. Regulators will ask.

Human: "who clicked this?"Agent: attributable, auditable, reversible

Why now

The capability arrived before the controls did.

Models can finally take real action. Boards are demanding adoption. And the safety, identity and audit layer to do it responsibly simply doesn't exist yet — so every enterprise is building a fragile version of it by hand.

Tool-use

Agents can now reliably call APIs and take multi-step actions — the technical blocker that held this back for years is gone.

Board-level

AI adoption is now a mandate from the top of the org, not an experiment in a lab. The pressure is to ship, safely.

No standard

There's no accepted way to give an agent governed access to enterprise systems. Whoever defines it owns the category.
