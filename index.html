The pitch in one line
Working concept — a thinking page, not a product page

Your bank runs on tribes.
Your money runs through all of them.

Every tribe — conventional, digital, finance — builds its own system to run its own work. So processes get trapped inside those systems, and no one can trace a single dollar from disbursement to revenue to product to income stream. Throughline is the rail where a process runs once, across apps, and every step is attributed end to end — done by people today, and by agents where it actually helps.

The reality today

Each tribe built its own system. The process — and the truth — got trapped inside.

This isn't a tooling gap, it's a structural one. The org is split into tribes, and the software mirrors the org. A sale that starts in one tribe and earns revenue booked by another has no continuous thread connecting the two.

Conventional banking

Own system

Built its own loan origination system to run a manual sales process its own way.

conventional-LOS · sales, origination

Digital

Own system

Lives separately, with its own stack, its own flows, its own definition of a "customer".

digital-platform · onboarding, app

Finance

Picks up the pieces

Has to calculate revenue when disbursement happens and revenue is collected — and map it back to product and income stream, by hand.

spreadsheets · reconciliation, GL
⌁

Between these systems there is no shared thread. The process is re-keyed, the data is reconciled after the fact, and the answer to "what did this product actually earn, and through which income stream?" is always late, manual, and arguable.

The three symptoms

What the silos cost you.

01

Duplicated systems

Every tribe rebuilds the same primitives — customers, products, approvals — in its own walled system, because connecting to someone else's was never an option.

02

Trapped processes

A sales or origination process is locked inside the app that runs it. You can't see it end to end, hand it across tribes, or change it without touching that one system.

03

No attribution

Because nothing carries a continuous thread, finance can't trace disbursement → revenue → product → income stream without rebuilding the story by hand, every month.

The idea

One rail. The process runs on it — and so does the attribution.

Instead of each tribe trapping a process in its own system, the process is designed as swimlanes and executes on a shared rail that connects your existing apps. Because every step crosses the rail, every step is tagged at the source — who did it, for which product, which income stream. Attribution stops being a reconciliation project and becomes a property of the rail.

On the rail

The tags were written when each event fired. Revenue by product, by income stream, by tribe is a live query — not a monthly reconstruction.

Humans and agents

Same rail, same rules — whoever does the step.

The rail doesn't care whether a step is done by a person or an agent. That's the point: you can automate a lane step the day it makes sense, without changing the process, the tracking, or the controls around it.

👤 People do the judgment

Credit decisions, exceptions, sign-offs — the steps that need accountability and discretion stay with humans, with the context delivered to them on the rail.

  • High-stakes actions route to the right person to approve
  • Nothing changes about who is accountable for a decision
  • The human sees the whole thread, not a fragment

🤖 Agents do the throughput

Document checks, pre-fills, reconciliation, recognition — repetitive steps an agent can run, under the same identity, permission and audit rules as any actor on the rail.

  • Every agent action is attributed and reversible
  • Scope, limits and approvals are enforced by the rail
  • Start with one lane step; expand as trust grows

Why it compounds

Attribution isn't the destination. It's the first turn of a flywheel.

Every step that runs on the rail records how work actually flows across the tribes. That data lets you see the real process, generate a better one and run it, then automate the routine lanes — which produces more data, and tightens the loop. Most tools can observe or act. Owning the rail means you do both.

How it actually gets built

Don't sell the rail. Sell the attribution — then the rail builds itself.

Telling tribes to put their processes on a shared platform is a political war. Solving finance's revenue-attribution pain is a budget line with one owner. Land there; the instrumentation you add to answer it is the first stretch of rail.

PHASE 1 · NOW

Attribution wedge

Instrument the origination-to-revenue flow for one product line. Give finance live disbursement → income-stream attribution. One owner (CFO), measurable value, no politics.

PHASE 2 · NEXT

The rail & the analytics

The tagged events become a shared thread and an analytics layer. Model the process as swimlanes on top of it so it lives on the rail, not one tribe's app — and start seeing the real process.

PHASE 3 · THEN

Generate processes

With enough coverage, generate the standardized process from the data and deploy it back onto the rail. The platform stops describing work and starts shaping it.

PHASE 4 · LATER

Automate middle office

Hand the routine, internal, high-volume lanes to agents — attributed, governed and reversible by default, one lane at a time.

Intellectual honesty

What has to be true for this to work.

The point of this page is to pressure-test the idea, not to sell it. These are the bets that decide whether it lives.

SPONSOR

Someone has authority across the tribes.

A shared rail asks tribes to give up a little control. Only the CFO (who feels the attribution pain) or someone above all tribes can mandate it. Without that sponsor, this stalls at the first silo wall.

WEDGE

The attribution pain is acute enough to fund a wedge.

If finance can live with the monthly spreadsheet, there's no urgent first sale. The bet is that "we don't really know what each product earns" is a board-level problem, not an annoyance.

MOAT

The moat is the closed loop, not a feature.

Mining tools observe but can't act; workflow tools act but don't observe the truth. Owning the rail means you do both — observe the real process, generate a better one, run it, observe again. That loop, fed by proprietary cross-tribe data, is what no single incumbent can copy.

ADOPTION

Connecting to legacy is hard, but it's the moat too.

The work is in the connectors and in getting a second tribe to put a process on the rail. Every process that moves onto it makes leaving costlier — but the first few are pure uphill.
